IDEA TITLE
RoadSide: a roadside mechanic in your phone that listens to your vehicle, looks at the problem and tells you what to do, fully on-device

TITLE ALTERNATIVES
- RoadSide: your phone listens to your vehicle and looks at the problem, then tells you what may be wrong and what to do, for bikes today and cars next, fully on-device
- RoadSide: hold your phone near your vehicle and it listens to the noise, looks at the problem, and tells you what may be wrong and what to do, fully on-device


DESCRIPTION
Smart living doesn't stop at the front door: for millions of people the device they rely on most every day is their bike or car, and when it starts making a new rattle, knock, click or squeal they have no idea whether it is something serious or nothing at all. You cannot type a sound into a search box, breakdowns rarely happen where there is signal, and the only options are to ignore it or pay a mechanic just to find out. RoadSide turns the phone you already carry into a roadside mechanic, with no dongle and no extra hardware. Hold the phone near the vehicle to record the noise, then point the camera at the problem area: lightweight on-device AI listens for faults such as chain rattle, brake squeal, engine knocking, misfires and belt squeal, and looks for visible problems such as a dirty chain, worn sprocket teeth or a corroded battery terminal. RoadSide combines what it hears and sees into one plain-language answer: what it found, what it may mean, what you can do right now, the tools you may need and a short safety note, such as riding slowly on smooth roads until a worn part is checked. An "Ask RoadSide" assistant answers follow-ups like "I don't have chain cleaner" or "Is it safe to ride?" using the current result. Everything runs on the handset: no account, no cloud and no internet connection, so it works in airplane mode, keeps recordings and photos private and costs nothing to run. RoadSide is designed as a trustworthy first check, not a replacement for a mechanic: it keeps what it observed separate from what it concludes, says "not clear enough" instead of guessing, and tells you plainly when it is time to get professional help.
